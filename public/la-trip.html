📍 Waypoints & Rest Stops

Checkpoints along I-5 south of San Mateo, in order — fuel up strategically, the desert stretch between Kettleman City and Buttonwillow runs thin on gas.

🥩 Harris Ranch / Coalinga

~167 mi · ~2h–2h15

Best mid-trip fuel + full meal stop. Shell truck center with gas, food court (Denny's, Carl's Jr., Burger King), plus the famous Harris Ranch Inn & Restaurant — ranch-raised steaks. Hotels on both sides of I-5 if you want to break the drive overnight.

🍦 Kettleman City

~200 mi · ~3h

The "middle of California" small-town stop. A pilgrimage-worthy In-N-Out on SR-41, Doc Burnstein's ice cream, and Bravo Farms for cheese/snacks. Gas stations at the SR-41 exit — a good quick stretch-and-fuel break.

⛽ Buttonwillow

~260 mi · ~4h

Big truck-stop services: TA Travel Center + "I-5 Oasis" (Sinclair gas, food court incl. Krispy Kreme, Starbucks, McDonald's) — one of the last full-service stops before the mountain pass. Local eats nearby: Mi Lindo Manzanillo, Tacos El Paraiso.

🍕 Bakersfield

~263 mi · ~4h–4h10

Largest city on the route — full downtown dining, gas everywhere, ATMs, hotels. Known for thick-crust pies (Flame's Pizza, Zeppo's). Fill up here — fuel gets scarce between Bakersfield and Palmdale.

⛰️ Grapevine / Tejon Pass Summit

~283 mi · ~4h20

Summit rest areas (both directions) with views into the San Gabriel Mountains and Antelope Valley. Weather watch: high winds/fog can close this pass — the wind advisory above applies right here.

🏁 Santa Clarita → Downtown LA

~327–370 mi · ~5h–5h40

The final stretch. Note: California mileposts count down as you head south, so signage will read decreasing numbers from Harris Ranch to the Grapevine.

  • Refuel at Harris Ranch or Bakersfield — both have full truck-stop service.
  • Best single-meal-stop plan if you only want to stop twice: lunch at Harris Ranch, last break at Bakersfield.
🗺️ Alternate Scenic Routes
US-101 Central Coast → Santa Barbara

~430–460 mi, roughly +1h–1.5h over direct I-5 (~7h total). Passes Monterey & Carmel, an optional Big Sur detour, Pismo Beach, and Santa Barbara.

Highway 1 Full Pacific Coast Highway

Monterey → Big Sur → Hearst Castle/San Simeon → Santa Barbara → LA. ~500 mi, 10–12h+ of driving (+4–6h). Best as a multi-day trip — watch for Big Sur road closures.

US-395 Sierra Side-Trip

Exit I-5 at Fresno, run down US-395 past Sequoia/Kings Canyon National Parks, rejoin the Valley near Visalia/Tulare. +1h–2h. Giant sequoias and mountain scenery as a break from the flat Central Valley.

📅 A Relaxed 3-Day Itinerary

One area per day to keep driving minimal — add ~30 min for LA traffic between zones. Most sights below are free or low-cost; only planetarium shows and museum special exhibits carry extra fees.

1

Hollywood & Griffith

Stars, sign views, and a hilltop sunset
🎬

Hollywood Blvd & TCL Chinese Theatre

Handprints, footprints, and movie-star plaster hands in the forecourt (6925 Hollywood Blvd). 30–45 minutes is plenty.

⭐

Walk of Fame

The star-studded sidewalk runs both directions along Hollywood Blvd, past the old TCL and El Capitan marquees. One hour max — it's kitsch by design.

🔭

Griffith Observatory (sunset)

Free admission with postcard views of the city basin and Hollywood Sign. Arrive 60–90 min before sunset. Lot fills by midday — park early or use the Greek Theatre lot.

🥗
Food pick:

Sqirl (Silver Lake) for brunch — L.A.'s most famous casual brunch spot: vegetable-forward plates, house-made pasta. Expect a line; go early.

2

Santa Monica & Venice

Beach day — walkable or a short bike ride
🎡

Santa Monica Pier (go early)

Amusement rides, the giant Ferris wheel with ocean views. Mornings are cool and quiet; by midday it's a proper crowd.

🚲

Coastal bike path to Venice

Rent bikes at the pier, ride ~2.5 flat scenic miles west to Venice (10–15 min). The best "I'm actually in L.A." moment on this trip.

🏖️

Venice Boardwalk & Muscle Beach

Street performers, tarot readers, a skate park, and the outdoor gym where locals lift by the surf. No admission — just walk end to end.

🎨

Venice Canals + Abbot Kinney Blvd

A 10-minute wander through the painted-house canals, then a slow stroll down Abbot Kinney: boutiques, vintage shops, cafés, food trucks.

🍕
Food pick:

Gjusta (Abbot Kinney Blvd) — wood-fired pizzas, pastas, huge shared plates. Books out fast on weekends; go off-peak (~10am). Alternative: Gjelina across the street.

3

DTLA, The Broad & Koreatown

Food hall, modern art, and a KBBQ send-off (+ optional Getty)
🌮

Grand Central Market

L.A.'s historic open-air food hall on Broadway (daily 8am–9pm). 40+ vendors — tacos, ramen, oysters, pastries. Free entry, best $20 meal in the city.

🖼️

The Broad

Free general admission modern art museum (closed Mondays) — Warhol's Marilyn dioramas and a huge contemporary collection. Walk-ups available; reserve online for guaranteed entry.

🎭

Arts District & Walt Disney Concert Hall

15-min walk south into warehouse galleries, murals, and craft-beer bars, looping past the Gehry-designed Concert Hall — one of L.A.'s best photo spots.

🏛️

Optional: The Getty Center

Free admission (timed-entry reservation recommended), art collections in a Renzo Piano complex with sweeping city views. Parking $25/car ($15 after 3pm).

🥩
Food pick:

Korean BBQ in Koreatown — Parks on Western Ave for a sit-down grill-your-own dinner, or Kingyubu nearby for cheap-and-filling yubu pockets.

🏙️ Neighborhoods to Know

Hollywood

The postcard version of L.A.: the Blvd, the Sign, the studios' shadow. Tourist by day, surprisingly lively dining scene by night.

Santa Monica

Polished beachfront city with a working pier and Montana Avenue's upscale shops — the friendliest entry point to coastal L.A.

Venice Beach

Chaotic, colorful, gloriously weird: street performers, canals, skate culture, and the best $10 lunch on Abbot Kinney.

Downtown LA (DTLA)

The city's urban heart — museums, the food-hall scene, concert venues, and a skyline that surprises first-time visitors.

Koreatown

Dense, loud, and endlessly hungry-friendly: KBBQ, late-night snacks, bakeries, and some of the best value food in the city.

Silver Lake

The indie pocket just north of Hollywood: quirky shops, famous brunch lines, and a reservoir that's L.A.'s unofficial living room.

📝 Practical Notes
  • Traffic: Between zones (Westside ↔ DTLA), budget 30–45 min in peak hours even for short drives.
  • Parking: Observatory lot fills by early afternoon — arrive before noon or use the Greek Theatre lot. Getty parking is cashless.
  • Hours: The Broad is closed Mondays; Grand Central Market runs daily 8am–9pm; Griffith Observatory is best at sunset.
  • Reservations worth making ahead: Sqirl and Gjusta on weekends, plus any sit-down K-Town dinner for groups of four or more.
